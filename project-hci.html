Usability testing

Abstract

On Tuesday, October 21st, 2025, group members David, Eli, Favour, Tyrone, and Divine conducted a usability test on a Japanese travel website Hakone Japan, to test the functionality and usability of the website. The purpose of this report is to provide our observations, collected data, analysis, and interpretation of our findings, and use this data to improve design decisions that directly affect users. We tested 10 participants in the adjunct faculty offices on the second floor of the York Building, from 12:30 pm to 1:45pm. Our process included each member of the group testing the website, creating questions to ask the participant before and after the test, compiling tasks to give the participants, generating a consent form, discussing key metrics to gather for our data collection, assigning roles to each member to ensure smooth operations during test day, multiple dry runs the day before to prepare for the test, and finally, carrying out the test. By the end of our report, we hope to have demonstrated our understanding of conducting a usability test through our data accuracy, mitigation of biases, and methodologies, and highlight how invaluable gathering real data from real users can be for improving user experience and guiding evidence-based design decisions.

Description of Website Evaluated

The Hakone Japan website, developed by the Hakone Tourist Association, is designed as a one-stop shop for tourists looking to plan a visit to Hakone. The home page features a slideshow showcasing some of Hakone’s landscapes, attractions, and cultural events, with the text “Hakone: Experience Wonder” displayed in the middle of the slideshow. Tabs “Home”, “Home”, “Discover”, “Things to Do”, and “Plan Your Trip” are located in the top right-hand section of the website, along with a search bar icon located in the top right corner. The image below is what users are first presented with when visiting the website.

Moving down the home page, users are presented with the title: “A place to experience and connect with nature and history”, some text below the title, and block sections that correspond to different areas of the website. These block sections 2 and 3 describe tourist information such as “Hakone Digital Map/Traffic Information”, “Summer in Hakone 2025”, “Hakone Sustainable Tourism”, “Hakone Local Tour”, and “Hakone Live Views”.



As we continue scrolling down the home page, we come across other relevant sections such as Discover Hakone, Things to Do,Plan Your Trip, and Hakone Travel Ideas. These subsections host important information for tourists about where to find activities, transportation, experiences, and places to stay

Besides the home page, there are many other pages and links that users can visit when interacting with the website. However, the home page is the most important, as it’s the first page users are presented with.

Logistics and Methodology of the Usability Testing

summary

The study was conducted on October 21st, 2025. Each study was considered started when the participant handed in both their consent and pre-questionnaire form. We began the study with the first participant at 12:26 PM and finished with the last participant at 1:54 PM. The study was conducted in room 2.. on the 2nd floor of 7800 York Rd. The purpose of our study was to test the usability and functionality of the Hakone, Japan tourist website. At the beginning of each test, we first greeted the participant and thanked them for joining our usability test. We then briefed the participant on the procedure of the study and what was to take place during the study. We reassured each participant that they are not being tested and that they are free to leave at any moment without penalty. Then we had the participant fill out a consent form and answer a short pre-questionnaire form. Once completed, the data completion team stepped in and took over. The data collection team conducted the study with each participant while noting observations and recording performance metrics. Upon completion of the study, the data collection team took the participant to David who was waiting in the hallway, to complete the post-questionnaire.
